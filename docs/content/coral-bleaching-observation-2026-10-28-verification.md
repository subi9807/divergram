## Environmental and safety cross-checks

The article was independently written after checking authoritative guidance that bleaching is a stress response and does not automatically mean coral death; bleached coral may recover when conditions normalize; temperature and sunlight can contribute to heat stress; in-water reports help ground-truth satellite products; trained and local reporting programs request both bleaching and no-bleaching observations; and divers should maintain buoyancy, avoid touching, collecting or relocating reef life, and follow local protection rules:

- NOAA Ocean Service, What is coral bleaching?: https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/coral_bleach.html
- NOAA Coral Reef Watch, Monitoring Coral Reef Conditions in the Field: https://coralreefwatch.noaa.gov/satellite/education/monitoring.php
- NOAA Coral Reef Watch, Current Global Bleaching Status Update and Data Submission: https://www.coralreefwatch.noaa.gov/satellite/research/coral_bleaching_report.php
- Florida Keys National Marine Sanctuary, Marine Heatwave and Coral Bleaching Response FAQs: https://floridakeys.noaa.gov/coral-bleaching-faqs.html
- Great Barrier Reef Marine Park Authority, Coral bleaching: https://www.gbrmpa.gov.au/learn/reef-health/coral-bleaching
- Great Barrier Reef Marine Park Authority, Responsible Reef Practices: https://www.gbrmpa.gov.au/access/responsible-reef-practices

The public guide does not invite divers to diagnose disease, collect samples, tag coral, apply adhesive, shade, transplant or perform treatment. It directs them to distinguish direct observation from interpretation, preserve distance and buoyancy, use the designated local reporting method, protect sensitive coordinates, and follow authorized gear-cleaning guidance without mixing improvised chemicals.
