## Safety and environmental cross-checks

The article was independently written after checking authoritative guidance that water, plant fragments, eggs and other small biological material can move on recreation equipment; dive gear should be checked, cleaned, drained and fully dried between waters; porous or damp equipment deserves particular attention; and site-specific biosecurity or disease-control instructions take priority:

- NOAA Repository, Stop Aquatic Hitchhikers field guide, including diving and swimming equipment: https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/41959/noaa_41959_DS1.pdf
- NOAA Coral Disease and Health Consortium, precautions for diving gear exposed to potentially diseased sites: https://cdhc.noaa.gov/outbreak-investigation/precautions/
- New Zealand Ministry for Primary Industries, why Check, Clean, Dry matters when moving between waterways: https://www.mpi.govt.nz/outdoor-activities/boating-and-water-activities-preventing-the-spread-of-pests-weeds-and-diseases/check-clean-dry-help-protect-new-zealands-waterways/why-check-clean-dry-when-moving-between-waterways
- New Zealand Ministry for Primary Industries, biosecurity considerations for outdoor equipment: https://www.mpi.govt.nz/outdoor-activities/tramping-tips-to-prevent-spreading-pests-and-diseases/biosecurity-considerations-walking-and-tramping
- NOAA National Marine Sanctuaries, clean, drain and dry guidance for preventing invasive-species spread: https://sanctuaries.noaa.gov/visit/fishing/best-practices/responsible-fishing.html

The public guide intentionally does not prescribe a universal bleach concentration, water temperature or contact time. Those requirements vary by organism, jurisdiction and equipment material, and an improvised treatment can damage life-support gear or the environment. Readers are directed to current local rules, official decontamination protocols and manufacturer care instructions, with regulator internals and technical-equipment servicing reserved for qualified professionals.
