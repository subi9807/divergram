## Safety cross-checks

The article was independently written after checking authoritative guidance on suspected decompression illness, emergency medical contact, trained oxygen first aid, preserving dive and symptom history, professional evaluation even after apparent improvement, and avoiding improvised in-water recompression:

- Divers Alert Network, Decompression Illness: https://dan.org/health-medicine/health-resources/diseases-conditions/decompression-illness-what-is-it-and-what-is-the-treatment/
- Divers Alert Network, Treating Decompression Sickness: https://dan.org/health-medicine/health-resource/dive-medical-reference-books/decompression-sickness/treating-dcs/
- Divers Alert Network, Treating Decompression Sickness (The Bends): https://dan.org/safety-prevention/diver-safety/divers-blog/treating-decompression-sickness-the-bends/
- Divers Alert Network, Emergency Oxygen for Scuba Diving Injuries Student Handbook: https://dan.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/08/dan-emergency-oxygen-handbook-v3.pdf
- NOAA Diving Standards & Safety Manual, emergency procedures: https://www.omao.noaa.gov/sites/default/files/documents/NDSSM%20Rev%2005%20May%202020.pdf

The public guide does not diagnose a condition, prescribe medication, teach neurological examination or oxygen administration, recommend a specific chamber, or provide an in-water recompression procedure. It directs potentially life-threatening cases to local emergency medical services, limits first aid to the provider's training and local rules, and requires professional evaluation rather than treating symptom improvement as clearance to dive.
