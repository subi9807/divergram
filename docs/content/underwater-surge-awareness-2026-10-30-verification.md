## Safety and environmental cross-checks

The article was independently written after checking authoritative guidance that currents can increase effort and breathing-gas use; divers should receive a site briefing, stay with the buddy, monitor gas, and avoid fighting the water; unsafe conditions are grounds to terminate a dive; and loss of control around a reef can lead to physical contact and coral damage:

- Divers Alert Network, Diving in Currents: https://dan.org/alert-diver/article/diving-in-currents/
- Divers Alert Network, Current Dives: https://dan.org/alert-diver/article/current-dives/
- Divers Alert Network, The Importance of Buoyancy Control: https://dan.org/alert-diver/article/the-importance-of-buoyancy-control/
- NOAA Diving Standards & Safety Manual, Diving Standards and Procedures: https://www.omao.noaa.gov/sites/default/files/documents/NOAA%20Dive%20Standards%20%26%20Safety%20Manual%20%28NDSSM%29.pdf

- British Sub-Aqua Club, Safe Diving Guide: https://www.bsac.com/safety/safe-diving-guide/going-diving/

The public guide deliberately does not prescribe reef grabbing, reef hooks, emergency ascent details, rescue maneuvers or a universal shore-exit technique. It directs divers to stop nonessential tasks, preserve open-water clearance, communicate with the buddy, reassess effort and gas, avoid narrow or overhead terrain, and end the dive through training and the site-specific operator briefing when control or visibility declines.
