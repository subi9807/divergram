## Safety and stewardship cross-checks

The article was independently written after checking authoritative guidance on preserving artifacts in situ, retaining archaeological context, checking local laws and permits, avoiding contact and artifact removal, using good buoyancy, responsible photography and reporting discoveries through competent authorities:

- UNESCO Code of Ethics for Diving on Submerged Archaeological Sites: https://www.unesco.org/sites/default/files/medias/fichiers/2025/03/uch_code_of_ethics_en.pdf
- NOAA Office of National Marine Sanctuaries, Dive Into Sunken History: https://sanctuaries.noaa.gov/visit/maritime-heritage/
- NOAA Lake Ontario National Marine Sanctuary, Shipwrecks and responsible recreational access: https://sanctuaries.noaa.gov/lake-ontario/heritage/shipwrecks/
- U.S. National Park Service, Underwater Sites: https://www.nps.gov/subjects/oceans/underwater-sites.htm
- U.S. National Park Service, Apostle Islands diving regulations and resource preservation: https://www.nps.gov/apis/planyourvisit/diving.htm
- U.S. National Park Service, Isle Royale scuba-diving rules and wreck hazards: https://www.nps.gov/isro/planyourvisit/scuba-diving.htm
- U.S. National Park Service, Submerged Cultural Resources FAQ: https://www.nps.gov/slbe/learn/historyculture/submerged_faq.htm

The public guide does not provide artifact-recovery, excavation, metal-detecting or wreck-penetration instructions. It explicitly separates external observation from overhead-environment entry, requires appropriate training, equipment, team planning and authorization for any penetration, and defers ownership, access, photography, mooring and reporting requirements to current local law and the responsible authority.
