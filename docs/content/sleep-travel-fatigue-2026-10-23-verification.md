## Safety cross-checks

The article was independently written after checking authoritative guidance that sleep deprivation can impair attention, reaction time, judgment and decision-making; demanding travel can disrupt sleep; divers should be rested and should not feel compelled to dive when unwell or conditions are unsuitable; physical and medical readiness should match the expected workload; and acute illness or prolonged loss of strength warrants delaying a return and considering medical evaluation:

- Divers Alert Network, A Timeline of Decompression Stress in Diving: https://world.dan.org/alert-diver/article/a-timeline-of-decompression-stress/
- Divers Alert Network, Health Status: https://dan.org/safety-prevention/return-to-diving-safely/health-status/
- Divers Alert Network, Travelers Medical Guide - Fitness to Dive: https://dan.org/health-medicine/travelers-medical-guide/planning-and-preparedness/fitness-to-dive/
- Divers Alert Network, Physical Fitness for Diving: https://dan.org/alert-diver/article/physical-fitness-for-diving/
- CDC Yellow Book, Scuba Diving: Decompression Illness and Other Dive-Related Injuries: https://www.cdc.gov/yellow-book/hcp/environmental-hazards-risks/scuba-diving-decompression-illness-and-other-dive-related-injuries.html
- CDC Yellow Book, Adventure Travel: https://www.cdc.gov/yellow-book/hcp/travel-for-work-other/adventure-travel.html

The public guide does not prescribe a universal number of sleep or recovery hours, claim that caffeine reverses sleep loss, diagnose unusual fatigue, or teach emergency treatment. It separates ordinary travel tiredness from acute illness and unusual postdive fatigue, encourages prompt professional evaluation when concerning symptoms accompany fatigue, and states that rest does not replace training limits, conservative planning, gas management, buddy procedures or the site briefing.
