## Safety cross-checks

The article was independently written after checking authoritative guidance on gradual motion-sickness symptoms, horizon reference, reducing close visual tasks and head movement, choosing a stable part of a vessel, rest and hydration, medication side effects that can impair diving, and avoiding entry while symptoms or side effects reduce safe performance:

- U.S. CDC Yellow Book, Motion Sickness: https://www.cdc.gov/yellow-book/hcp/travel-air-sea/motion-sickness.html
- U.S. CDC Travelers' Health, Motion Sickness: https://wwwnc.cdc.gov/travel/page/motion-sickness
- Divers Alert Network, Seasickness Prevention and Treatment: https://dan.org/safety-prevention/diver-safety/divers-blog/seasickness-prevention-and-treatment/
- Divers Alert Network, Motion Sickness: https://dan.org/health-medicine/health-resources/diseases-conditions/motion-sickness/
- U.S. Coast Guard, A Boater's Guide to the Federal Requirements for Recreational Boats, including the warning not to confuse carbon monoxide exposure with seasickness or intoxication: https://uscgboating.org/assets/1/AssetManager/Boaters-Guide-to-Federal-Requirements-for-Receational-Boats-20231108.pdf

The public guide does not prescribe a medicine, dose, timing schedule or universal treatment. It tells readers not to trial or share medication on dive day, highlights impairment as a no-dive reason, and directs personal medication questions to a clinician or pharmacist. It also avoids treating every headache, dizziness or nausea as motion sickness, because exhaust exposure, heat illness, dehydration, medication effects and post-dive illness can require different urgent action.
